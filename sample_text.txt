Silent bit rot can corrupt archived photographs, tax records, and code
repositories without any warning sign appearing on the screen. Hamming
codes solve this by weaving mathematical redundancy directly into the
stored bytes, so a single flipped bit can be located and repaired later
without ever needing a separate backup copy sitting on another disk.

=== DATA INTEGRITY MATTERS ===

Consider a hospital storing patient X-ray images digitally. If a single
bit flips in the image header, the entire file may become unreadable.
If it flips in the pixel data, a tumor shadow could vanish or appear
where none exists. This is not science fiction — NASA's Voyager probes
detected approximately 1 bit error per day in their onboard memory from
cosmic ray bombardment during the Jupiter flyby.

Financial institutions face similar risks: a flipped bit in a bank
balance stored on disk could change $1,000,000 to $1,000,001 or $0.
Without error-correcting codes, there is no way to detect this change.

Error-correcting codes like Hamming(7,4) provide a mathematical safety
net. By adding just 3 parity bits for every 4 data bits, any single-bit
error in a 7-bit block can be automatically located and corrected —
no human intervention, no backup copy needed.
